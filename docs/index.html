What has happened so far

    A YouTube video hosts this universe: Conway's Game of Life on 128×72 cells with wrapping edges, one generation every two minutes, around the clock. The video's thumbnail shows the grid and its title shows the status. What you see above is the same universe, computed in your browser from the bot's latest state.

    Every comment on the video injects cells. Left alone, a finite universe must eventually return to a state it was in before, and from then on it is stuck in a loop forever. When that happens a 24-hour countdown starts. If no comment breaks the loop in time, the universe dies: an obituary video is uploaded and the original goes private.

    Every comment changes this universe. Some save it. Some kill it.

    How to comment

    glider x,y [ne|nw|se|sw]a glider (default se)
    block x,y · blinker x,y · rpent x,y · acorn x,ya pattern with its top-left corner at (x, y)
    cells x,y x,y …up to 25 single cells
    a pattern's name on its own: block, glider ne, acornthat pattern, at a spot picked from your comment
    anything elsea 5×5 random patch derived from a hash of your comment
    add red, orange, yellow, blue, purple, pink or cyanyour cells in that color, here, on the live stream and in the Shorts (only paint: the game plays out the same)

    x is 0–127 and y is 0–71, from the top-left. Comment as often as you like: up to 3 moves per person count each generation (every 2 minutes), and the rest are skipped. New comments are picked up within about 5 minutes (live chat within about 3); for two hours after a comment lands, everything it changed is drawn in white (and anything it killed outlined in red), boxed, with its command beside it.

    Verifiable

    Every run is recorded in a hash-chained public log, and anyone can replay the whole life from the seed to check it. The source, the full rules and the log are on GitHub.

    Visits and button presses are counted anonymously with GoatCounter: no cookies, no personal data.
